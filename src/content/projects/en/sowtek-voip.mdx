## Problem

Sowtek's contact centres run their calls on Asterisk. The PBX emits a constant stream of low-level events (channels, bridges, queues, hang-ups, recordings) that mean nothing to an agent or a supervisor until they are turned into calls, queues and outcomes. Supervisors also wanted to know how calls went, not just how long they lasted.

## Constraints

- **PBX events are noisy**: one call is dozens of events across channels and bridges, sometimes out of order. {/* CHECK */}
- **Real time**: agents and supervisors see calls, queues and agent states live.
- **Saudi dialect**: sentiment needs reliable transcripts first, from the [Saudi-dialect speech-to-text](/projects/saudi-stt) model.
- **On-premise options**: government clients run the stack inside their own networks.

## Key decisions

1. **Turn PBX events into domain events early.** Everything downstream works with calls, queues and agents, never with raw Asterisk channels.
2. **Transcribe, then classify.** Sentiment runs on dialect-aware transcripts, so it understands what the customer actually said.
3. **Calls join the customer's history.** A call, its transcript and its sentiment sit next to the customer's chats and tickets.
